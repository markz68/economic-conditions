Sources & comparison rules

This describes conditions during a term, not proof of presidential causation. Congress, the Federal Reserve, inherited conditions, and global events also matter. There is no overall partisan score.

Monthly baseline: December before inauguration. Quarterly baseline: Q4 before inauguration. Weekly baseline: last observation before inauguration. Annual fiscal baseline: last fiscal year ending before inauguration. Deficits are shown positive; surpluses negative. Fiscal years span administrations and are not attributed wholly to one president.

Quarterly dates identify the quarter’s start; annual dates identify the fiscal year. Only fully completed observation periods are included. Same-point mode uses elapsed calendar days from the focus term’s inauguration, capped at each term’s end and today. Revisions can change historical comparisons; this uses latest-revised history, not data vintages known at the time.

Inflation uses matching months 12 months apart. Cumulative prices reset to zero at each term’s baseline. Rate changes are percentage points; other levels show percentage change. Payroll levels are jobs, not people. Real earnings and real disposable income are inflation adjusted in the source series’ dollar bases; disposable income is an annualized per-person average, not median household income. Mortgage rates exclude other ownership costs.

Charts show up to five years ending at the selected endpoint; they may include the preceding term. Green Better and red Worse badges describe generally favorable directions vs the inherited level, not presidential credit or a direct ranking of administrations. Arrows show the numerical direction. Recent direction uses the latest observation versus 3 months earlier (monthly), 13 weeks earlier (weekly), the prior quarter, or the prior fiscal year; dates are shown and may cross a term boundary. Little changed means less than 0.1 percentage point for rates or 0.5% for levels, a display convention rather than statistical significance. Overall inflation and GDP growth need context. Cumulative prices use descriptive higher/lower wording. Debt and deficit judgments require fiscal context. Each card links to its FRED series and its original publisher’s methodology. Missing values remain unavailable.

Settings & About

Install from your browser’s app menu, or use Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen on iPhone.

Offline mode retains the last downloaded dashboard and observations. Online “Check for updates” downloads the latest published snapshot; it does not run FRED collection.

No account or cloud sync. Preferences are stored only in this browser. Export is a read-only snapshot; restore is unnecessary because official data is fetched again.
